## Slab lifting in Amarillo

Void formation under Amarillo slabs is a predictable consequence of the clay soil. When Panhandle clay dries out, which it does every drought cycle, it shrinks away from the underside of the slab. The result is an air gap: a void between the concrete and the now-lower soil surface.

Concrete that spans a void has no support. When the load is applied, foot traffic, furniture, vehicles in the garage, the slab section drops or cracks. By the time it's visible, the void has usually been there for months.

Slab lifting fills the void and restores grade without tearing out and replacing the concrete.

## What slab lifting can fix in Amarillo homes

- **Interior floor sections**: rooms where one side of the floor is noticeably lower than the other, or where transitions between rooms have a sudden step that wasn't there before
- **Garage floors**: settled garage slabs that slope toward the house instead of away, or that have dropped at the threshold
- **Driveways**: settled driveway panels that create water pooling or a trip hazard
- **Exterior steps and walkways**: settled concrete steps and path panels around the perimeter

Slab lifting is appropriate for settled sections that are still structurally intact. Concrete that has deteriorated, cracked through multiple times, or is too thin is typically a replacement rather than a lift. We tell you which situation you have.

## When slab lifting isn't the right fix

If the foundation wall itself has moved, settled, bowed, or cracked, that's a pier installation problem, not a slab lifting problem. Lifting a slab that continues to move because the foundation wall is still settling is a temporary measure. We don't sell it as a permanent fix.
