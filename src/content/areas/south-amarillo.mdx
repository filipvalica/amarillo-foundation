## Foundation repair in South Amarillo

South Amarillo is one of the city's older residential neighborhoods, a mix of pre-1980 homes, 1980s ranch-style construction, and some later development. The housing stock in the working-class areas south of downtown is predominantly slab-on-grade construction from a period when reinforcement standards were lower and drainage engineering was minimal.

These homes have been through every Panhandle drought cycle since they were built. For a 1965 slab, that's 60 years of clay shrinkage and re-expansion. The cumulative movement from those cycles is significant, and many South Amarillo homes show the long-term result: widening corner cracks, doors that have gradually stopped latching, floors with visible slope that wasn't there when the house was new.

The affordability of South Amarillo's housing market also means deferred maintenance is common. Foundation problems that would have been addressed earlier in a higher-value market have sometimes been lived with for years.

## South Amarillo foundation profile

- **Long-term differential settlement**: 40–60 year old slabs that have accumulated movement across multiple drought cycles; the pattern of cracks and slope tells the settlement history
- **Active movement post-drought**: South Amarillo homes on heavier clay subsoils show more acute movement after drought cycles end; the delayed re-expansion hits older slabs harder
- **Plumbing-related settlement**: older homes with aging cast iron slab plumbing see additional foundation movement from slow plumbing leaks that saturate soil locally; we check for drainage anomalies that might indicate an under-slab leak
- **Stair-step brick cracks**: exterior brick on South Amarillo homes shows clear differential settlement history; the stair-step crack pattern through mortar joints marks where one section has moved relative to adjacent sections
