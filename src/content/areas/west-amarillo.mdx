## Foundation repair in West Amarillo

West Amarillo's housing stock is primarily post-2000 construction, newer suburban development that extended the city's footprint west along the I-40 corridor. Homeowners in West Amarillo are sometimes surprised to see foundation cracks in homes built in 2005 or 2010. Newer construction is supposed to be built better, and in many respects it is. But it's still sitting on Panhandle clay.

Modern construction standards account for expansive soils through reinforced slab design and vapor barriers, but those measures reduce, not eliminate, the effects of Amarillo's shrink-swell soil. A severe drought cycle like 2011–2014 or 2022–2023 pushes clay movement beyond what reinforced slabs are designed to handle in isolation.

West Amarillo's newer homes also have larger footprints than the older central neighborhoods. More perimeter means more exposure to drainage variation, and drainage on large-lot suburban homes is often site-specific rather than engineered.

## Foundation issues in West Amarillo

- **Drainage-caused clay movement**: landscaping installed for aesthetics rather than engineering sometimes routes water toward foundation perimeters; this is the most correctable problem in the neighborhood
- **Post-drought settlement**: West Amarillo homes built in the mid-2000s hit their first major drought cycle test during 2011–2014; homes that settled then may have accumulated additional movement in subsequent cycles
- **Garage floor settlement**: large garage slabs on post-2000 homes are common first indicators; they're at grade and respond to soil changes before the main slab does
- **Pre-purchase inspection requests**: West Amarillo's active resale market means we regularly handle foundation condition reports for buyers due diligence before closing
