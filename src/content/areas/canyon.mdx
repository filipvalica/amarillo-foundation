## Foundation repair in Canyon

Canyon is the county seat of Randall County, approximately 15 miles south of Amarillo on US-87. It's home to West Texas A&M University, a small-city downtown, and a mix of older residential construction and newer development driven by families who prefer Canyon's smaller scale and WTAMU proximity to Amarillo's urban core.

The soil profile in Canyon is essentially the same as Amarillo, caliche-laden Panhandle clay that expands when wet and contracts during drought. Canyon is part of the same regional drought zone as Amarillo, which means the 2011–2014 and 2022–2023 drought cycles affected Canyon homes on the same timeline.

One distinction: Canyon's residential stock includes more of the 1950s–1970s ranch construction that is highest-risk for accumulated drought damage, alongside newer development near the WTAMU campus and along the US-87 corridor.

## Common foundation problems in Canyon

- **Mid-century slab settlement**: older ranch homes near downtown Canyon show the same accumulated drought damage pattern as South Amarillo; 50+ year old slabs on caliche clay without drainage correction are the highest-risk inventory in the market
- **Newer development near WTAMU**: student and faculty housing near the university represents a higher-turnover market where foundation condition is often not evaluated between owners; landlord-owned properties in Canyon are a regular part of our workload
- **Drought-driven movement on rural-edge lots**: Canyon's semi-rural lots with larger yard areas and less irrigation than suburban Amarillo see the clay dry-cycle more completely; the contrast between wet and dry extremes is higher on larger, less-irrigated lots
