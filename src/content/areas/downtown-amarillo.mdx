## Foundation repair in downtown Amarillo

Downtown and central Amarillo contain the city's oldest residential and commercial structures, buildings from the early 1900s through the mid-century period. The Texas Panhandle Builders Association is headquartered here, and the construction trades have deep roots in this part of the city.

Residential properties in the central neighborhoods range from Victorian-era homes near the historic district to 1940s–1950s ranch-style construction. These buildings represent a different foundation challenge than the post-1970 slab construction in the outer neighborhoods: some sit on pier-and-beam foundations that are susceptible to wood rot and soil movement, others on early poured concrete foundations that predate modern reinforcement practices.

The mixture of commercial and residential use in central Amarillo also creates unusual foundation conditions. Neighboring commercial structures with different footings, utility corridors under the streets, and decades of variable drainage infrastructure all contribute to foundation movement patterns that can be harder to read than a standard suburban slab.

## What we find in downtown Amarillo

- **Pier-and-beam foundation deterioration**: older homes on pier-and-beam construction need periodic beam inspection and wood replacement; soil moisture under raised floors in the central area has affected beam integrity on some historic properties
- **Early poured concrete foundation cracking**: pre-1950 foundations used lower-grade concrete mixes that are more prone to deterioration and cracking under cumulative load
- **Commercial adjacency effects**: building vibration, utility corridor excavation near a residential foundation, and differential loading from neighboring structures contribute to movement in some central properties
- **Foundation evaluation before renovation**: downtown Amarillo properties being renovated for residential or mixed-use conversion should have a foundation evaluation as part of the project scope; we work with architects and contractors on phased evaluations
